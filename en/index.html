National Taipei University of Technology Institute of Mineral Resources Engineering

Rock Laboratory

We investigate the scientific problems of rock mechanics, advance the practice of geotechnical engineering, and provide better solutions for resources engineering.

Research Topics

Rock Mechanics

Rock is a natural material, and its mechanical behaviour differs greatly from that of the man-made materials we usually deal with. From a microscopic point of view, the arrangement of the mineral grains governs how cracks develop once the rock is loaded; from a macroscopic point of view, the distribution of joints within a rock mass has a decisive influence on its stability and on its behaviour in engineering works. Understanding the mechanical behaviour of rock is therefore the core objective of this laboratory.

Numerical Analysis

As the demand for development grows, engineering designs become ever more complex, and conventional closed-form calculations can no longer satisfy the needs of practical analysis. Numerical analysis has become an indispensable tool in geotechnical and resources engineering: it allows the safety of a design to be examined by simulation, and the risks involved to be identified in advance.

Engineering Geology

Geotechnical works have to be built on natural materials such as rock and soil, and the properties of natural materials vary enormously. Anyone who wishes to sculpt the natural world must first understand its geology. Engineering geology, the bridge between geotechnical engineering and the geological sciences, is therefore a research topic of great importance.

Geotechnical and Resources Engineering

Geotechnical engineering covers a very wide field: foundation excavation, slope stability, dam analysis, tunnelling, and the investigation and remediation of landslides all belong to it. Every one of these areas is closely bound up with people's daily lives, which makes it an industry with strong ties to society as a whole.

Apparatus

The apparatus of this laboratory is used mainly to investigate the strength, deformability and other mechanical properties of rock materials. Our central instrument is the RTX-1000 triaxial rock testing system made by GCTS, USA, with which the common tests on rock — uniaxial compression and triaxial compression — can be carried out. The laboratory also owns a rock core cutter, which trims drilled cores to the size required for subsequent testing. Small instruments related to rock mechanics, such as a point load tester, an ultrasonic pulse velocity tester and a Schmidt hammer, are available for use in the field. In addition, the laboratory holds a range of surveying instruments, including levels, theodolites and total stations.
