Geotechnical and Resources Engineering

Geotechnical engineering covers a very wide field: foundation excavation, slope stability, dam analysis, tunnelling, and the investigation and remediation of landslides all belong to it. Every one of these areas is closely bound up with people's daily lives, which makes it an industry with strong ties to society as a whole. “Every piece of construction has to have its foundation laid by geotechnical engineering. Before Taipei 101 could be built, its piles had to be driven; before the high speed railway could be constructed, slopes had to be stabilised and tunnels excavated. We are, in a sense, the vanguard of Taiwan's major infrastructure.” Beyond that, the techniques and theories of geotechnical engineering are closely bound to related fields such as mineral extraction and geological investigation.

For those in geotechnical and resources engineering, Taiwan is a place well worth exploring. Our geological setting is remarkably varied: this small island holds structures and lithologies of every kind. We also have a distinctive stone industry, and Taiwanese technology is well advanced in both quarrying and processing. Because the geology is young and fragile, and because rainfall and earthquakes are severe, the demand for specialists in geotechnical engineering and hazard mitigation is very large indeed.
